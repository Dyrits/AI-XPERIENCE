Nodal

Ernst Chladni, 1787 · sand, a bow, a plate

Chladni sprinkled sand on a plate, drew a violin bow across its edge, and found the sand gathering into figures: the lines where the plate does not move. They are the nodal lines, and they exist only while the plate rings at one of its natural frequencies.

This is that experiment, rebuilt. The plate is a square held at its rim, and its mode shapes are the classical sums and differences of cosines, so every boundary is a nodal line and the sand gathers along the edge as well as across the figure. The sand is not painted — every grain is pushed down the gradient of the squared displacement, so it crawls into the nodal lines and piles up there on its own. A damped wave equation runs underneath, so strikes and the bow are genuine transients crossing the metal.

Tune the drive frequency to a resonance and the ring-up meter fills, the figure darkens and the plate sings. Off resonance the sand barely stirs. Switch a mode from its difference to its sum, or mix two of them, and the interference figures arrive. The scope draws the resonance curve you are hunting along.
