Beatloom

a loom that weaves what you play

Six instruments sit on six concentric rings. Tap a step to make that instrument play on that beat — the pattern loops every 16 steps, like a bar of music. Every beat that fires weaves a thread into the tapestry at the centre of the screen, and every full loop lays down one more ring, so the rug grows outward the longer you keep the loom running.

Kick and snare beats become hubs: anything else that fires nearby draws a faint chord back to them, so a simple rhythm slowly grows into a woven, spirograph-like pattern that is yours alone. Nothing you do can sound wrong — everything is tuned to one key.

Beatloom

a rhythm loom

Beatloom is a step sequencer whose music is also its own portrait: six synthesized instruments — kick, snare, hat, pluck, bass and chime — sit on six rings around a centre point. Toggle steps to build a loop; the loom plays it, and every fired step draws a glowing thread onto a tapestry that never resets, so the piece you compose becomes a piece of generative art at the same time.

Kick and snare hits are the loom's two hubs. Anything else that fires draws a faint chord of thread back to the most recent hub, which is what turns a plain beat into a web of crossing lines over time. Every full 16-step loop pushes the whole ring band one notch further from the centre, like a rug growing row by row, up to a point where it settles and simply keeps brightening.
